Notes from my own P&L

Five things I learned by spending real money on them. Each one ends with something you can try this week.

A point of view is a filter

Two AI companies launched this year on opposite readings of the same fact. In June, Hang Ten raised $32M betting AI eats IT services. Six weeks later June came out of stealth with $20M, arguing that deploying AI is now the bottleneck, which creates a new kind of services work. Both will sell, because a point of view finds the buyers who already half-believe it.

We learned the same thing selling air purifiers to offices. Specs and pricing got ignored. What got replies was an email showing a CXO the air quality reading at their own office that morning.

Try this: Write the one sentence your best-fit buyer already half-believes, and put it above everything else on your homepage.

Make the technical proof the reason they buy

Consultancies were selling enterprises the same carbon outcome we were, as a service. So we built a measurement platform that used satellite data and field sensors to verify results, and put it at the front of every pitch.

Try this: List the proof points a larger competitor cannot copy in six months. Lead with the first one.

The number that decides a category is rarely in the market report

We almost built an alkaline water business and killed it in two months. The reason was one number no report carried: 30 to 40% of every Kangen machine's price goes to distributor commissions, roughly $1,800 to whoever closes a household. Our bottle was going to sell for $100 to $120. You cannot pay someone $1,800 to close a sale that small, so the channel that built the category was closed to us.

Try this: Before sizing a category, find out who gets paid to close each sale, and how much.

Flow revenue is capped by trigger volume, so twelve flows won't save you

The standard advice says get twelve email flows live. In our account, four flows produced 88% of flow revenue. The bigger leak was two flows fighting over the same trigger, where the older one wins by default: the flow earning $3.08 per recipient got 177 sends while the one earning $0.66 got 16,810.

Try this: Export your flow report, sort by revenue per recipient, and check whether any two flows share a trigger. In B2B the same cap applies to trial and onboarding sequences.

An agent is only as good as the context you give it

I answered 40+ questions about myself before I let an AI write a single line for me. You would give a new hire six weeks to learn how you sell. An agent gets only what you put into words, so the ceiling on its output is how carefully you have examined your own work. My whole operation now runs on a folder of plain markdown files that any AI can read.

Try this: Before your next agent project, write down the ten questions a new hire would ask you in week one, and answer them.

About

I've spent seven years selling things made in one place to buyers in another: air purifiers to offices in Delhi, carbon credits from Indian farms to companies in Europe and the US, a hydration brand to American families. The move that worked every time was the same one, finding the proof a buyer can't ignore and building the whole pitch around it.

I work in bursts, and anything I have to do twice becomes a system. Mornings go to training and meditation, which is most of the reason the bursts don't burn me out.

Principles

  1. 1

    Choose the field slowly, then refuse to leave it.

    A channel pays in its second year, and most teams switch in the first.

  2. 2

    I don't invent things; I carry things across.

    My best wins moved a proof from one world into another, like a lab module into a sales deck.

  3. 3

    When you know what's coming, prepare. When you don't, position.

    Launches get plans. New categories get a point of view and room to move.

  4. 4

    Always ask what you can avoid.

    Four email flows made 88% of the revenue. The work was deciding what to switch off.

  5. 5

    Technology is an efficiency layer on the value you offer.

    I build agents to take work off a team, and the offer still has to be worth buying.

  6. 6

    Relationships compound longer than any deal.

    I would rather lose a negotiation than a person I will work with again.
